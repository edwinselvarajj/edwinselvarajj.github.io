Nbyula verdict

Strong for an intensive Asia-focused MBA, if the compressed format suits your career plan.

The main tradeoff is pace. A one-year format reduces time away from work but leaves less room for career experimentation.

Where the placement number stops agreeing with itself

Cohort, year, source and limitations travel with every metric. When two credible sources disagree, both are printed.

NTU, self reported92 to 95%

Employed within three months. The school publishes 92%, 93% and 95% across three of its own pages for the same cohort.

Financial Times, audited65%

Same programme, same ranking year, independently audited for the Global MBA Ranking 2026.

What is genuinely hard about this programme
  • The employment figure is unresolved. A thirty point gap between the school’s own number and the audited one is the single most important thing on this page, and no competitor page in the benchmark mentions it.
  • Twelve months is short for a career pivot. The compressed format removes the long internship that a career changer usually relies on.
  • Round three decides on 15 May against a 3 August start. That leaves the Student’s Pass as the binding constraint, not the admission.

SourceFinancial Times, Global MBA Ranking 2026, Nanyang programme data·checked 7 Aug 2026

Who should shortlist it?

The close is a decision aid, not a generic conclusion.

This may suit you if

  • You have clear post-MBA goals in Asia.
  • You can manage a compressed calendar.
  • You value an applied one-year format.
  • Your funding plan covers the full cost.

Look elsewhere if

  • You need a long internship window.
  • You want a two-year campus experience.
  • Your goal depends on unsupported outcome claims.
  • Your budget requires a lower total cost.
